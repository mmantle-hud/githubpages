# Introduction to Week 7

This week we finish off the main relational database part of the course by looking at three different topics: views, indexes and transactions. 

A view is an SQL `SELECT` statement that is saved inside the database under a specific name. There are two main reasons for using a view. To help us manage complexity when our statements grow very large, and for security, to hide sensitive data. 
We will look at examples of views, and understand how to create views in Postgres. 

Indexes are data structures stored alongside our tables that are designed to speed up selecting rows. We will look at the widely used B-tree index, explore the potential disadvantages of indexes, and consider when we should and shouldn't use indexes in our databases. 

Finally, we will look at concurrency and transactions. How relational databases handle many users performing actions at the same time, and how we can group statements to run as a block. 

There are fewer labs to work through this week, but you will get some hands-on experience of views and indexes. I think last week was fairly challenging, so having a bit less lab work should give you chance to catch-up anything you didn't manage to complete.
